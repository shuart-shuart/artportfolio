Hong Shu-ying 方舒颖 is a hoarder of images and stories. Her practice examines how meaning drifts through imperfect systems of copying and circulation, and what persists in the gaps, mistranslations, and contradictions between copies.

Working across books, prints, and moving images, her practice moves between two strands: one rooted in scores, annotations, and embodied memory; the other in hoarded images and circulated media. She works with handwritten notations and personal archives shaped by nearly two decades in schools and amateur Chinese Orchestras, and images made and circulated by others. From there, she traces how fragments and contradictions form personal and collective knowledge.

Alongside her practice, she develops collaborative platforms that extend these interests into shared contexts. She co-founded Part Time Book Club, a collective that hosts informal gatherings for sharing art books. Driven by an interest in what others collect, it works to expand public access to art books in Singapore. She also initiated | | on paper, a resource-lean, self-initiated residency structured around conversations and four A4 sheets. Conceived in response to limited access to formal residencies, it offers a parallel space for dialogue, reflection, and iteration.

Hong Shu-ying received the Kwek Leng Joo Prize of Excellence in Photography and was featured in the 6th Women in Film and Photography Showcase by Objectifs Centre for Photography and Film (Singapore). She has presented solo exhibitions at Esplanade (Singapore, 2024) and Yao Alternative Space 窯座 (Taichung, 2024), with an upcoming presentation at RM Gallery (Auckland, 2026). Her work has also been shown at Three Shadows Xiamen Photography Art Centre and Pingyao International Photography Festival (China); Jakarta International Photography Festival (Indonesia); PRINTROOM (Netherlands); CICA Museum (South Korea); Pon Ding 朋丁 (Taiwan); and DECK, I_S_L_A_N_D_S Peninsula, Art Outreach, and ADM Gallery (Singapore). She holds a BFA (Hons) in Media Art (Photography) from the School of Art, Design and Media, Nanyang Technological University, Singapore.

She is currently a resident at the Singapore Art Museum.
